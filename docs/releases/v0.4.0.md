# Kubby v0.4.0

Kubby's fourth public preview is about network policy and safer day-2
operations: see which Pods a NetworkPolicy isolates and whether a route is
actually blocked, understand a crash from the logs written before the restart,
and see what a drain or a manual scale will really do before confirming it.

## Highlights

- Browse HorizontalPodAutoscalers, PodDisruptionBudgets and NetworkPolicies as
  first-class sections. An autoscaler that cannot scale and a budget that
  currently allows no eviction are flagged; a policy states whether it denies or
  allows all traffic. Their drawers link to the scaled workload or the Pods they
  protect and select.
- Read the logs from before a container's last restart. The Logs tab opens on
  the restarted container, says how its last instance ended (for example
  "restarted 7 times · last exit OOMKilled, exit 137, 3m ago"), and switches to
  the previous instance in one click. AI evidence now includes those logs.
- Check whether NetworkPolicy lets one Pod reach another Pod or a Service on a
  port. Both sides are evaluated — egress from the source, ingress to every Pod
  behind the Service — and the verdict (allowed, blocked or partial) names the
  policies responsible.
- See blocked routes in Topology. Traffic is evaluated from the Istio ingress
  gateway, or from the ingress-nginx or Traefik controller identified through the
  IngressClass; a route that policy blocks becomes a broken path naming the
  policy. Every Service offers Check access, and isolated Pods are marked.
- Copy the equivalent `kubectl describe` or `kubectl logs` command from the
  drawer.

## Safer operations

- Drain previews its impact before confirmation: PodDisruptionBudgets that will
  refuse evictions and stop the drain partway, Pods with no controller that will
  not be recreated, and emptyDir data that will be deleted.
- Scale warns when a HorizontalPodAutoscaler manages the Deployment, because the
  autoscaler overwrites a manual scale at its next sync, and links to it.
- Incident Studio's "Inspect current and previous logs" action now leads to logs
  that include the previous instance.

## Performance and release quality

- NetworkPolicy selectors are compiled once per request. With 100,000 endpoint
  rows and 50 policies the Traffic view went from 5.3 s to 24 ms, a traffic check
  against 1,000 Pods and 500 policies from about 1 s to 41 ms, and topology memory
  returned to its previous level.
- Routes are not evaluated at all when the viewed scope has no NetworkPolicy, so
  clusters without policies pay nothing for the new checks.
- Deterministic allocation-budget and request-count tests guard the policy paths;
  the kind smoke now covers the new CLI commands (`get hpas|pdbs|networkpolicies`,
  `logs --previous`, `netpol-check`, `containers`, `drain-impact`).

## Download

Download `kubby-windows-amd64.exe` and
`kubby-windows-amd64.exe.sha256` from the assets below. Windows 11 normally
already includes the required WebView2 runtime. Kubby needs a kubeconfig with the
permissions for the read and write operations you choose to perform.

## Preview limitations

- The Windows executable is not code-signed, so Windows SmartScreen may ask you
  to confirm the publisher before first launch.
- This prerelease publishes a Windows x86-64 binary only; source remains portable.
- NetworkPolicy results evaluate the policy objects, not packets: whether they are
  enforced depends on the cluster's CNI, and CNI-specific policies,
  AdminNetworkPolicy and service-mesh authorization are not evaluated.
- Blocked-route detection recognises the ingress-nginx and Traefik controllers
  and the Istio ingress gateway. Other controllers are reported as not evaluated;
  use Check traffic from the controller Pod. In a namespace-scoped view with no
  NetworkPolicy, egress policy in the controller's namespace is not evaluated.
- The drain preview reads PodDisruptionBudget allowances at preview time; they
  can change while the drain runs.
- Browser automation cannot prove native WebView2 clipboard, focus/paint, file
  dialog, ANSI rendering, or PTY resize behavior; those remain native release
  checks.
- `govulncheck` reports GO-2026-5932 in Helm v3's linked legacy OpenPGP
  package, for which upstream provides no fixed version. Kubby does not expose
  that provenance-verification path, explicitly keeps `ChartPathOptions.Verify`
  disabled, and instead binds reviewed Helm writes to SHA-256 preview digests;
  a regression test pins this boundary.
- Helm, Artifact Hub, and hosted AI providers require outbound network access.
